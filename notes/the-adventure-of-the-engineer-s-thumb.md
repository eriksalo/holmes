# The Adventure of the Engineer's Thumb

## What happens
A railway guard brings Watson a new patient at dawn: Victor Hatherley, a young hydraulic engineer whose thumb has been hacked off. Hatherley, two years in business with takings of £27 10s., had been offered fifty guineas by a gaunt German calling himself Colonel Lysander Stark to examine a hydraulic press at a secret country house, on condition of absolute secrecy and a midnight arrival. Driven in a carriage with frosted windows, warned to flee by a terrified woman, he insisted on finishing the job, recognised that the "fuller's-earth" story was a lie, said so, and was locked inside the press as it descended. The woman opened a side panel and saved him; Stark struck off his thumb with a cleaver as he hung from a window; he fainted among roses and woke by the station. Holmes deduces the house is six miles from Eyford, not twelve, because the horse was fresh. They arrive to find it in flames (Hatherley's lamp, crushed in the press). The coiners have fled with their boxes and are never caught. Hatherley has lost a thumb and a fee; Holmes offers him "experience."

## What the work observes about being human

1. **Need and flattery together override every warning instinct.**
   - Where it is spoken: Hatherley: "I bowed, feeling as flattered as any young man would at such an address." And: "A feeling of repulsion, and of something akin to fear had begun to rise within me at the strange antics of this fleshless man. Even my dread of losing a client could not restrain me from showing my impatience." And: "I thought of the fifty guineas, and of how very useful they would be to me."
   - What the action does with it: Hatherley notices every wrong note (the secrecy, the midnight hour, the questions about his being an orphan, the fee "at least tenfold what I should have asked") and goes anyway. He "threw all fears to the winds, ate a hearty supper, drove to Paddington." The story confirms that a man can see danger clearly and walk into it because he is poor and has been told he is trusted. The woman's warning, "There is no good for you to do," is refused twice.

2. **Stubbornness is a virtue that looks exactly like folly from the outside.**
   - Where it is spoken: Hatherley: "But I am somewhat headstrong by nature, and the more ready to engage in an affair when there is some obstacle in the way... Why should I slink away without having carried out my commission, and without the payment which was my due?"
   - What the action does with it: His refusal to leave nearly kills him; his refusal to accept the fuller's-earth story ("I was admiring your fuller's-earth") is what provokes Stark. But the same stubbornness is what makes him examine the trough and find the metallic deposit, and what makes him hesitate at the window to see whether the woman is ill-used. The story does not reward or punish the trait; it shows it cutting both ways in the same hour.

3. **A man will be chosen for a crime precisely because no one would miss him.**
   - Where it is spoken: Stark: "I have it from the same source that you are both an orphan and a bachelor and are residing alone in London." And: "we may expect that more from a man who is alone than from one who lives in the bosom of his family."
   - What the action does with it: Holmes's cutting about the missing engineer Jeremiah Hayling, "aged twenty-six," shows this is a method, not an accident. Stark's compliment on discretion is a selection criterion for murder. The story confirms that isolation is not only a sorrow but a vulnerability, and that the language of trust can be an inventory of who can be disposed of.

4. **The woman inside the criminal house is both its conscience and its prisoner.**
   - Where it is spoken: Elise: "For the love of Heaven! get away from here before it is too late!" And later, to Stark: "Fritz! Fritz! remember your promise after the last time. You said it should not be again. He will be silent! Oh, he will be silent!"
   - What the action does with it: She saves Hatherley twice, and her line "after the last time" is the story's quiet confession that she failed to save Hayling. Stark's reply, "You are mad, Elise! You will be the ruin of us," treats mercy as a liability. She escapes with the gang; the story never gives her a surname or a fate. The action confirms that one person's decency can operate within a murderous enterprise without being able to stop it, and that this person pays by staying.

5. **Cruelty that is not personal is the hardest to understand.**
   - Where it is spoken: Watson: "What I cannot understand is why they should have spared you when they found you lying fainting in the garden." Hatherley: "I never saw a more inexorable face in my life." Holmes on Stark: "like those out-and-out pirates who will leave no survivor from a captured ship."
   - What the action does with it: The footprints in the mould answer Watson: Hatherley was carried to safety by "one of whom had remarkably small feet and the other unusually large ones," which Watson reads as the woman and "the silent Englishman, being less bold or less murderous than his companion." Stark would have finished him. The story divides the gang into three temperatures of cruelty and shows that the victim's survival depended on which two of the three found him first.

6. **Justice may be served by accident when it cannot be served by law.**
   - Where it is spoken: Holmes, watching the fire: "Well, at least, you have had your revenge upon them. There can be no question that it was your oil-lamp which, when it was crushed in the press, set fire to the wooden walls." Watson: "those criminals were not destined to fall into the hands of justice."
   - What the action does with it: The coiners escape entirely: "from that day to this no word has ever been heard either of the beautiful woman, the sinister German, or the morose Englishman." Their machine is destroyed by the very weapon they used, lit by the man they tried to kill. The story offers this as a kind of recompense and knows it is not one. Holmes's "ingenuity failed ever to discover the least clue."

7. **Suffering has a market value only if it can be told.**
   - Where it is spoken: Hatherley: "I have lost my thumb and I have lost a fifty-guinea fee, and what have I gained?" Holmes: "Experience... you have only to put it into words to gain the reputation of being excellent company for the remainder of your existence."
   - What the action does with it: The story's own existence is the proof: Watson opens by noting that the tale "has, I believe, been told more than once in the newspapers," and here it is again. Holmes's answer is a joke and also exactly true. The engineer with no clients now has a story. The reader is left with the cold arithmetic that the only thing Hatherley gets for his thumb is the right to tell this.

## What it teaches
This is the story in which the detective is almost beside the point. Holmes contributes one deduction (the fresh horse means six miles, not twelve) and arrives to find the house burning and the criminals gone. Watson says as much in the first paragraph: the case "gave my friend fewer openings for those deductive methods of reasoning." What the story has instead is a man telling you, in order, every moment at which he could have turned back and did not, and why. Hatherley's narration is unusually honest about the small vanities and large needs that moved him: the flattery of being called discreet, the fifty guineas, the pride that made him sneer about fuller's-earth to a man holding a key.

What it leaves you with is a kind of ledger. One thumb, one fee, one night. Against that: a story, a fire, and the knowledge that the woman who saved him is still with the man who maimed him. Holmes's closing joke is the only consolation on offer, and the story does not pretend it is adequate. A reader may feel that this is the plainest account in the collection of what it costs an ordinary person to brush against organised wickedness: not death, not ruin, but a permanent small loss and a story that other people will enjoy more than he does.

- "I suppose that everyone finds his first independent start in business a dreary experience."
- "we may expect that more from a man who is alone than from one who lives in the bosom of his family."
- "'I beg that you will state your business, sir,' said I; 'my time is of value.' Heaven forgive me for that last sentence, but the words came to my lips."

- "But I am somewhat headstrong by nature, and the more ready to engage in an affair when there is some obstacle in the way."

## Preoccupations
- need and flattery overriding instinct
- isolation as vulnerability
- the woman as conscience inside a criminal household
- justice by accident when law fails
- cruelty in degrees within a single gang
- suffering converted into story
